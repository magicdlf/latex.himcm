2012A: Elk Population Dynamics

Outstanding Solution Analysis

📅 Year: 2012 🏆 Outstanding Paper 🦌 Wildlife Management
← Back to Home

Problem Statement

The 2012A problem addressed elk population management and conservation strategies. Teams were challenged to model population dynamics, habitat requirements, and the impact of various management interventions on long-term herd sustainability.

Core Challenge: Developing comprehensive population models that balance ecological sustainability with human interests including hunting, agriculture, and conservation.

Outstanding Approach Analysis

1. Population Dynamics Model

The outstanding team created a sophisticated age-structured population model incorporating seasonal variations and environmental carrying capacity.

2. Key Innovations

  • Spatial habitat modeling with migration patterns
  • Predator-prey dynamics integration
  • Climate change impact projections
  • Adaptive management strategy framework

3. Mathematical Framework

The solution employed Leslie matrices for population projection combined with stochastic elements to account for environmental variability.

Critical Success Factors

  • Ecological Accuracy: Incorporation of biological realism and field data
  • Long-term Perspective: Multi-decade projections with uncertainty analysis
  • Stakeholder Consideration: Balancing conservation with economic interests
  • Adaptive Management: Flexible strategies responsive to population changes

Key Takeaways

This outstanding solution demonstrated mastery in:

  1. Building biologically realistic population models
  2. Incorporating uncertainty and stochasticity appropriately
  3. Connecting mathematical models to management decisions
  4. Communicating complex ecological concepts clearly

Lessons for Future Participants

Essential strategies from this outstanding paper:

  • Ground models in ecological theory and empirical data
  • Consider multiple spatial and temporal scales
  • Include sensitivity analysis for management parameters
  • Present results in formats useful for decision-makers
